## Why "zabuton"?

A *zabuton* (座布団) is the traditional Japanese floor cushion, and in
Japanese culture it carries two meanings at once.

The first is **hospitality**. Offering a zabuton to a guest says "you will
not be seated carelessly here — you are welcomed as someone who matters."
That single cushion on the tatami is the host's respect and care, made
visible.

The second is **recognition**. On *Shōten*, a long-running Japanese comedy
show, a zabuton is awarded on the spot for a great line — "a zabuton for
you!" is instant, feel-good applause the whole room shares.

Both come from the same root: a zabuton is how you hand someone the message
"we recognize that you are here, and we are glad" — not as an abstract
sentiment, but as a physical thing you can touch and sit on. Before any words
of praise or welcome, a seat is prepared for you.

The zabuton apps try to carry that spirit: small, tangible tools that welcome
you, sit quietly on your desktop, and make everyday moments a bit more
pleasant.
